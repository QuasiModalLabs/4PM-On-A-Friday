# Business context

Read by `review-to-hardstyle` to shape the Styles prompt and set the tone of the
output. Fill this in once. Update it when something changes.

## What the business is

<!-- One or two sentences. Trade, size, location if it matters.
     e.g. "Independent specialty coffee bar, two sites in Toronto, 12 staff.
     High volume weekday mornings, slower weekends." -->

## Who this is for

<!-- Internal fun / marketing / a specific campaign or event.
     This changes how sharp the output should be. Internal can be brutal;
     anything public should punch at the situation, not the customer. -->

## Voice

<!-- How the brand talks. Dry? Warm? Self-deprecating? The Styles prompt
     inherits this — a business that jokes about itself gets more absurd
     styling than one that doesn't. -->

## Recurring themes

<!-- Complaints or compliments that show up constantly. Naming them here helps
     the skill spot the ones worth turning into a track.
     e.g. "Wait times at peak. Oat milk running out. Staff warmth is the
     thing people mention most in positive reviews." -->

## Vocabulary

<!-- Product names, in-house terms, local references worth keeping verbatim in
     lyrics. Also anything to avoid. -->

## Off limits

<!-- Named staff (always stripped regardless), specific incidents, legal or HR
     matters, anything under dispute. Be explicit — the skill will otherwise
     treat a vivid complaint as good material. -->
